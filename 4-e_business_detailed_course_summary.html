Chapter 1

Introduction to e-Business

Defining electronic business scopes, categorizing digital transactions, and analyzing the legal and structural challenges within the Algerian ecosystem.

1.1 E-business vs. E-commerce vs. Social Commerce

E-business (coined by IBM in 1996) is the use of digital technologies to transform all key processes (buying, selling, internal coordination). E-commerce is specifically the buying and selling portion.

E-business

Scope: All processes, internal/external. Place: Intranets, ERPs, Extranets. Ex: A bank managing transactions via SATIM.

E-commerce

Scope: Commercial transactions. Place: Merchant sites. Ex: Paying on an approved DZ site via CIB.

Social Commerce

Scope: Transactions inside social apps. Driven by community trust (influencers, live video). Ex: Buying via Facebook/TikTok with Cash on Delivery.

2 Main Transaction Categories

B2B (Business-to-Business)

Largest by value. Wholesaler ordering from producer.

B2C (Business-to-Consumer)

Most visible. Online stores selling directly to public.

C2C (Consumer-to-Consumer)

Individuals trading. Ex: Ouedkniss classifieds.

G2C (Gov-to-Citizen)

Public services. Ex: Requesting civil documents.

3 AI in E-business

  • Recommendation Systems: Collaborative, content-based, or hybrid filtering to drive engagement.
  • Chatbots: Natural language order tracking/support.
  • Predictive Analytics: Demand forecasting and dynamic pricing.
  • Risks: Algorithmic bias, privacy (regulated by Law 18-07), and explainability.

5 & 6 The Algerian Legal Framework & E-commerce Challenges

Any student of e-business in Algeria must understand these three fundamental texts:

Legal TextKey Obligations & Effects
Law 18-05 (May 2018)
Electronic Commerce		 Supplier must be on commercial register. Website must be hosted in Algeria under a .dz domain. Must use approved payment systems. Bans online sale of alcohol, pharma, and intellectual property.
Law 18-07 (June 2018)
Data Protection		 Requires transparency, customer consent, and prior declaration to the ANPDP (installed Aug 2022). Mandates strong security measures.
Law 25-10 (July 2025)
Virtual Currencies		 Prohibits purchase, sale, use, and possession of virtual currency. Adds criminal penalties for mining or operating exchange platforms.

Why is E-commerce Limited Despite 76.9% Internet Penetration?

Informal Economy Merchants avoid card payments to keep turnover invisible/untraceable.
Cost of Terminals High perceived cost (commission, bank formalities) for low-margin retailers.
Habit & Trust Consumers prefer Cash on Delivery (COD) to see the product before paying.
Logistics/Addressing Imprecise addressing across a vast territory increases failed deliveries.
Digital Literacy Smartphone use does not equal secure online banking confidence.
Regulatory/Trade Import restrictions limit the variety of products offered legally online.

Despite this, 2025 saw a 179% growth in card payments, indicating a market in transition.

Chapter 2

E-business Network Technology Basis

Demystifying the underlying protocols, the client-server architecture, and the crucial security mechanisms defending electronic transactions.

1 The Three Networks

The same technologies build different networks distinguished by who is allowed to enter.

Internet

Open to everyone. Public presence, online sales. (The public square).

Intranet

Only members of one organization. Internal HR, docs. (Inside a building).

Extranet

Selected external partners with controlled rights. Joint planning. (A locked room for visitors).

Note: The World Wide Web is NOT the Internet. The Web (HTTP/URLs) is just one service running on the physical/logical Internet infrastructure, alongside e-mail and file transfer.

3 The 3-Tier Client-Server Model

1. Presentation Tier

Interface seen by user (HTML, CSS). Collects input.

2. Application Tier

Business logic (JS, Server code). Calculates price, discounts.

3. Data Tier

Databases storing products & stock. Strictly controlled.

Advantages: Security (DB hidden from net), Scalability, and Maintainability.

4 TCP/IP Protocol Suite & The 5-Step Load

LayerProtocolsE-biz Role

Opening an Online Shop

  1. DNS Resolution: Translate .dz name to numeric IP.
  2. TCP Connection: Open reliable connection.
  3. TLS Handshake: Agree on encryption keys (Padlock).
  4. HTTP Request: Browser requests page; server builds it.
  5. Display: Page renders, user interacts.

6 Security: Threats & Countermeasures

E-business pursues 5 goals: Confidentiality, Integrity, Availability, Authentication, and Non-repudiation.

Interception

Attacker reads data on network.

Defense: Encryption (SSL/TLS / HTTPS)

Phishing

False message imitates bank.

Defense: User education, One-Time Passwords (OTP)

Card-not-present fraud

Stolen numbers used online.

Defense: Tokenisation, 3-D Secure, Dynamic OTP

Malware / Ransomware

Software blocks company data.

Defense: Offline backups, restricted rights

Denial of Service (DoS)

Server flooded with requests.

Defense: Traffic filtering, scalable hosting

Legal Obligations (Law 18-07)

Must use appropriate tech measures (HTTPS, Access Control), collect minimal data, and declare processing to ANPDP.

Chapter 3

e-Business Model Ontology / Unbundling

Analyzing the architecture of value creation, the 9 building blocks of the Canvas, and why electronic networks force corporations to unbundle their core activities.

2 The Business Model Canvas (Osterwalder & Pigneur, 2010)

A business model describes the logic by which an organization creates, delivers, and captures value. It is not a strategy (which is competitive positioning). The Canvas maps 4 pillars into 9 blocks.

Product Innovation

  • Value Propositions: Problem solved / need satisfied. (Authentic regional crafts)

Customer Relationship

  • Customer Segments: For whom?
  • Channels: How do we reach them?
  • Customer Relationships: Trust mechanisms.

Infrastructure Mgt

  • Key Activities: What must we do?
  • Key Resources: What must we own?
  • Key Partnerships: Who supplies us?

Financial Aspects

  • Revenue Streams: What users pay for.
  • Cost Structure: Most important costs.

3 Unbundling the Corporation (Hagel & Singer)

Historically, high interaction costs (searching, negotiating, coordinating) kept three conflicting core businesses bundled inside one company. Electronic networks reduce these costs, allowing each activity to unbundle and specialize.

Core BusinessEconomic DriverCulture
Customer Relationship Mgt Economies of scope: More products to same customer. Service-oriented, relational.
Product Innovation Speed and talent: Being first > being large. Creative, risk-tolerant.
Infrastructure Mgt Economies of scale: High fixed costs / large volume. Standardized, cost-focused.

→ REBUNDLING →

Scale players dominate infrastructure/customers; small firms handle innovation.

3.2 Algerian Examples

Ouedkniss

Essentially a Customer-Relationship business. Value lies in audience size and trust. Driven by economies of scope.

SATIM

A pure Infrastructure business. Operates national card switch. Success measured by reliability and low unit cost of transactions.

Yassir

A hybrid combining Customer-Relationship (the app interface/users) with Infrastructure (the fleet dispatching system).

Chapter 4

IS-IT Models & E-payment

Aligning technology with business strategy, mapping the Algerian payment ecosystem, and securing the step-by-step flow of online card transactions.

1 & 2 IT vs. IS & Strategic Alignment

IT (Information Technology): The tools (hardware, networks).
IS (Information System): IT + users + procedures + rules. Buying technology does not automatically create a working IS (e.g., unused POS terminals).

Strategic Alignment Model (Henderson & Venkatraman)

Business Strategy (External) Scope, distinctive competences, partnerships.
Org Infrastructure (Internal) Structure, procedures, staff skills.
IT Strategy (External) Tech scope, governance, sourcing choices.
IT Infrastructure (Internal) Architecture, apps, data.

Requires Strategic Fit (External to Internal) and Functional Integration (Business to Tech).

3.1 The Algerian Payment Ecosystem

CIB card Domestic interbank card issued by banks.
Edahabia Issued by Algérie Poste. Most widely held in country.
SATIM Interbank operator. Runs national switch & online payment platform.
GIE Monétique Organizes, develops, and monitors the national system.
Visa / Mastercard Accessible for travel allowance (750 EUR) per 2026 regulations.

4 Step-by-Step Online Card Payment & Security

1
Order: Customer confirms basket. Protected by TLS (Encryption) in transit.
2
Redirection to Gateway: Merchant never stores card. Protected by Tokenisation.
3
Authentication: Cardholder proves identity to prevent card-not-present fraud. Protected by 3-D Secure / OTP (Non-repudiation).
4
Authorisation: Request travels through switch to issuing bank to check balance. Guarded by Fraud scoring AI.
5
Confirmation & 6. Settlement: Merchant confirms order. Funds transferred later minus commission. Ultimate trust backed by Chargeback procedures.
