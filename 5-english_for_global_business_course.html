Lesson 1

The Global Business Environment

Understanding the fundamental definitions, micro/macro factors, and the forces driving globalization and international trade.

1.1 Defining the Environment

The Business Environment refers to the conditions, factors, and forces that influence the activities, decisions, and development of an organization. A business does not operate in isolation.

Internal Environment

Factors within the organization influencing its performance.

Employees Management Corporate Culture Financial Resources

External Environment

Factors outside the organization influencing its activities.

Customers Competitors Government Economic Conditions

1.2 Micro vs. Macro Environment

Micro-Environment (Direct Effect)

Includes Customers, Suppliers, Competitors, Distributors, and Business Partners.

Macro-Environment (Broader Forces)

Analyzed using the PESTLE framework:

P - Political: Government policy
E - Economic: Inflation, unemployment
S - Social: Consumer lifestyles
T - Technological: AI, automation
L - Legal: Employment legislation
E - Environmental: Climate change

1.3 Globalization & Multinational Companies (MNCs)

Globalization refers to the increasing interconnectedness and interdependence of countries, economies, organizations, and people, driven by international trade, technology, and digital platforms.

Domestic vs. International Business

DomesticInternational
Operates primarily within one country.Conducts activities across national borders.
Single currency and legal system.Manages different currencies, cultures, laws, and political conditions.

Multinational Companies (MNCs)

An organization that operates in more than one country. An MNC may:

  • Produce goods in different countries.
  • Establish foreign subsidiaries.
  • Manage complex international supply chains.
  • Employ workers in different cultural settings.

1.4 Global Business: Opportunities vs. Challenges

Opportunities

  • Market Expansion: Reaching new international customers.
  • Access to Resources: Global suppliers, tech, and skills.
  • Economies of Scale: Large-scale production reduces average costs.
  • Diversification: Reducing dependence on a single domestic market.

Challenges (Threats)

  • Cultural Differences: Varied consumer behavior and business etiquette.
  • Currency Fluctuations: Exchange rate changes affecting profits.
  • Legal Differences: Complying with foreign regulations.
  • Political Uncertainty: Instability or economic crises abroad.

1.5 Language Focus

Essential grammatical structures for discussing global business:

Present Simple (Facts/Routines)

"Companies operate in competitive markets."

Present Continuous (Trends)

"Businesses are expanding into new markets."

Future Forms (Predictions/Plans)

"Global competition will increase."

Modals (Ability/Obligation)

"Companies must comply with local regulations, but they should analyse the market first."

Lesson 2

Listening Comprehension: Going Global

Analyzing a real-world case study on international expansion, focusing on the challenges faced by foreign founders and the keys to navigating a new business environment.

2.1 Case Study: Building a $1B Business

Based on the video presentation by John Kim, Co-founder of Sendbird.

Background & Context

  • Founder: John Kim, Co-founder of Sendbird.
  • Expansion Path: The company started in South Korea and decided to expand to the United States.
  • Reason: Seeking a larger global market and access to international investment opportunities.

Challenges Faced

As a foreign founder entering the U.S. market, John Kim faced significant hurdles, including language barriers, cultural differences in business etiquette, and a lack of an existing network. Having a good product alone was not enough to succeed without adapting to the new environment.

Key Lessons Learned

1. Customer Understanding

A company cannot simply copy its domestic strategy. It must deeply understand local customer needs and adapt the product accordingly.

2. Communication is Critical

Building relationships and communicating effectively with local stakeholders, partners, and customers is just as important as the technology itself.

3. The Reality of "Going Global"

Foreign founders often face unconscious biases and difficulties. Overcoming these requires resilience, careful planning, and a willingness to learn the unwritten rules of the new market.

Lesson 3

Reading: Doing Business in a Globalized World

Extracting key concepts regarding supply chains, macroeconomic influences, and vocabulary essential for international operations.

3.1 Text Analysis & Key Concepts

The Impact of Globalization

Globalization has increased the interconnectedness and interdependence of economies. A modern company may design a product in one country, source materials globally, manufacture elsewhere, and sell worldwide. This highlights the critical importance of global supply chains.

Multinational Companies (MNCs)

MNCs coordinate activities across markets to access new customers, technologies, and specialized skills. However, they face challenges such as managing international employees, complying with diverse legal systems, and adapting to local cultures.

Strategic Planning Requirement

Entering a global market requires careful analysis of the target market, competitors, regulations, and risk assessment. Success depends heavily on the organization's ability to understand and adapt to its macro-environment.

3.2 Business Vocabulary

  • Competitor: A rival company in the same market.
  • Supplier: Provider of goods/services.
  • Regulation: Official rules controlling business.
  • Stakeholder: Person/org affected by a business.
  • Market: System of exchange (buyers/sellers).

Lesson 4

Speaking & Written Communication

Applying theoretical knowledge through practical role-play, drafting professional recommendations, and designing an international market-entry strategy.

4.1 Speaking & Role-Play

Discussion: Is globalization more beneficial to large companies or small businesses?

Useful Expressions for Debate:
  • "In my opinion..." / "I believe that..."
  • "One major advantage is..." / "Another benefit is..."
  • "However..." / "One possible challenge is..."
  • "I agree because..." / "I disagree because..."

Role Play: Entering a Foreign Market

Student A: Business Manager

You want your company to enter a foreign market and are seeking strategic advice.

Student B: Consultant

Advise the manager on market opportunities, competition, culture, and regulatory risks.

4.2 Written Comprehension

Drafting a professional recommendation for market expansion (150-180 words). The document must clearly communicate:

A definitive stance (enter / do not enter).
Identification of the primary market opportunity.
Identification of the most significant challenge/risk.
Actionable steps to take prior to entry.

4.3 Practical Project: International Market Entry

A comprehensive 9-step framework for planning global expansion.

1. Profile

Define the company.

2. Target Market

Select foreign destination.

3. Justification

Reasons for expansion.

4. Customers

Identify target audience.

5. Competitors

Analyze local rivals.

6. Opportunities

Potential growth areas.

7. Challenges

Cultural & legal risks.

8. Resources

Financial/Human capital.

9. Strategy & Recommendation

Proposed entry mode and final verdict.
