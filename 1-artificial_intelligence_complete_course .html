1.3 What is Artificial Intelligence?

Artificial Intelligence refers to the branch of computer science concerned with designing systems capable of performing tasks that normally require human intelligence. Unlike traditional software which follows fixed instructions, AI systems improve their performance by analyzing data and adapting their behavior.

1.3.1 Core Characteristics of Artificial Intelligence

Learning

Improves performance by analyzing historical data to identify patterns instead of relying exclusively on predefined rules. (Ex: Sales forecasting)

Reasoning

Evaluates available information and applies logical procedures to generate conclusions or recommend actions. (Ex: Credit approval)

Problem Solving

Identifies optimal or near-optimal solutions to problems involving multiple constraints and objectives. (Ex: Supply chain optimization)

Perception

Recognizes images, speech, handwriting, and sensory input using computer vision and speech recognition.

Adaptation

Continuously improves predictive accuracy as additional data becomes available through repeated learning cycles.

Automation

Enables repetitive tasks to be performed automatically with minimal human intervention, increasing productivity.

1.11 Worked Technical Examples

Example 1: Rule-Based vs Classifier

Evaluating ticket urgency where h = hours waiting, u = unavailability status.

Rule: If h ≥ 24 OR u = 1 → Urgent
Model: z = -2 + 0.08h + 1.5u
Prob p = 1 / (1 + e^-z)

For h=10, u=1: Rule gives Urgent. Model gives z = 0.3, p ≈ 0.574 (Urgent if threshold is 0.50). Insight: Classifiers learn coefficients from data; threshold tuning balances missed alerts vs false alarms.

Example 2: Evaluating Predictions
Actually UrgentActually Normal
Pred Urgent18 (TP)12 (FP)
Pred Normal2 (FN)68 (TN)

Accuracy = 86%. Precision = 18/(18+12) = 60%. Recall = 18/(18+2) = 90%. Insight: Accuracy hides trade-offs (e.g., 40% of alerts are false alarms). Managers must assess the workload created by false alerts.

Types of Artificial Intelligence

Understanding the categories of AI helps managers recognize capabilities, limitations, and potential business impacts. (2.1 & 2.2)

2.3 Narrow AI (ANI)

Weak AI (Current Reality)

2.3.1 Characteristics

  • Designed for one specific task/domain.
  • Cannot transfer knowledge to unrelated problems.
  • Performs efficiently in structured environments.
  • No consciousness or self-awareness.

2.3.2 Examples

Siri/Alexa Netflix Recs Spam Filters Facial Recog.

2.3.3 Limitations

Cannot reason beyond training domain; requires human supervision for strategic oversight.

2.4 General AI (AGI)

Strong AI (Long-Term Goal)

The vision of developing machines capable of performing any intellectual task a human can accomplish. It would possess general reasoning abilities, transfer knowledge across domains, and learn continuously.

2.4.1 Future Prospects

Currently non-existent. Challenges include developing common-sense reasoning, transferring knowledge, understanding human emotions, and aligning AI objectives with human values.

2.5 Superintelligence (ASI)

Highest Theoretical Level

Machine intelligence that surpasses human capabilities across virtually all cognitive tasks (reasoning, creativity, problem-solving). Capable of continuous autonomous self-improvement.

Raises profound concerns regarding global safety, ethics, and human control.

2.9.1 Example: Recommendation Cosine Similarity

Learner preference vector q=(3,1). Course a=(4,0), Course b=(1,3). Dot product over magnitudes:

sim(q,a) = (3*4 + 1*0) / (√(3²+1²) * √(4²+0²)) = 12 / (4√10) ≈ 0.949
sim(q,b) = (3*1 + 1*3) / (√10 * √10) = 6 / 10 = 0.600

Interpretation: The system ranks Course A higher based strictly on supplied features. It does not possess AGI (understanding context outside the vector math).

Data consists of raw facts. Organizations transform this raw data into valuable insights, a process AI accelerates to generate actionable intelligence.

3.5 & 3.6 Types and Sources of Data

3.5.1 Structured Data

Predefined format (rows/cols), easy to query with SQL. Ex: ERP, CRM, Payroll DBs.

3.5.2 Semi-Structured Data

Contains tags/metadata providing partial structure. Ex: XML, JSON, HTML, Emails.

3.5.3 Unstructured Data

>80% of enterprise data. No predefined format. Processed via NLP/Vision. Ex: Images, Video, Text, Audio.

Sources (3.6): AI uses both Internal (ERP, CRM - highly controlled) and External (Social media, IoT, Gov DBs - broad insights) data.

3.8 Data Quality

"Garbage In, Garbage Out (GIGO)"

Dimensions of quality: Accuracy (real-world representation), Completeness (no missing info), Consistency (identical across systems), Timeliness (current), Relevance (supports objectives), Validity (conforms to formats).

3.9 Preprocessing Techniques

  • 3.9.1 Cleaning: Remove duplicates, handle missing values, correct typos.
  • 3.9.2 Integration: Combine separate datasets (e.g., merge CRM & ERP).
  • 3.9.3 Transformation: Normalization, scaling, encoding categoricals.
  • 3.9.4 Reduction: Feature selection, PCA to decrease volume & boost speed.

3.15 Worked Tech Examples & Case Study

Example 1: Missing Values & Scaling

Training data: 2, 4, missing, 10. Median = 4. Data becomes (2, 4, 4, 10). Min-Max scaling: x_scaled = (x - 2) / (10 - 2). Transformed: 0, 0.25, 0.25, 1.

Interpretation: Recomputing median or range on held-out records causes "data leakage". Apply training params to future data.

Example 2: Aggregation w/o Data Leakage

Predicting Feb purchase based on Jan data. Must NOT use early Feb transactions as features for the start-of-Feb prediction.

Interpretation: Define the feature window strictly before prediction time to ensure model validity.

Chapter 4

NLP and Generative AI

Transforming unstructured textual data into actionable knowledge, and advancing to systems capable of creating entirely new content. (4.1 & 4.2)

4.2 & 4.4 Natural Language Processing & Pipeline

NLP transforms flexible, ambiguous human language into structured numerical information for machine learning.

4.4.1 Collect

Gather emails, posts, reviews.

4.4.2 Clean

Remove HTML, punctuation, URLs.

4.4.3 Tokenize

Divide text into words/tokens.

4.4.4 Stop-Words

Remove frequent words (the, is).

4.4.5 Lemma.

Reduce words to base forms.

4.4.6 Extract

Convert to math vectors (TF-IDF).

Feature Extraction Deep-Dive (4.4.6)

  • Bag of Words: Raw word frequency count. Ignores context.
  • TF-IDF: Weights important keywords higher than common ones. Math: tfidf(t,d) = count(t,d) * ln(N/df(t)) (See Worked Example 4.14.1).
  • Word Embeddings: Represents words as dense numerical vectors. Words with similar meanings (King/Queen) are positioned close together in multidimensional space. (Word2Vec, BERT).

4.5 Major Tasks in NLP

4.5.1 Classification

Assigning categories to documents. Ex: Spam detection, routing support tickets.

4.5.2 Sentiment Analysis

Opinion mining to determine tone (Positive/Negative/Neutral). Ex: Brand reputation.

4.5.3 NER

Named Entity Recognition identifies people, orgs, locations. Ex: "Microsoft [Org] in Paris [Loc]"

4.5.4 Translation

Deep learning translation preserving meaning/context (Google Translate, DeepL).

4.5.5 Question Answering

Direct answers vs search lists. Ex: Enterprise knowledge chatbots.

4.5.6 Summarization

Generates shorter versions. Extractive (selects sentences) vs Abstractive (writes new ones via LLMs).

4.6 & 4.7 Generative AI & LLMs

Unlike Traditional AI (which classifies or predicts outcomes), Generative AI creates entirely new content (text, images, code) dynamically based on learned patterns.

4.7 Large Language Models (LLMs)

Trained on massive datasets (books, web). They do not retrieve answers from memory; they understand language structure.

4.8 How they generate text:

LLMs generate text one token at a time by predicting the most probable next token based on context (See 4.14.2 Softmax Temperature).

4.11 AI Agents

Systems capable of perceiving environments, reasoning, acting autonomously, and adapting via feedback to achieve complex objectives.

Designing effective instructions to guide Generative AI toward accurate responses.

  • Context: Provide background info.
  • Specific Instructions: Task details.
  • Format & Constraints: Tables, length, audience.
